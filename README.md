The recent announcement of the Photon Matrix product represents a relevant conceptual advance in mosquito control through optical means. The core idea—identifying insects in flight and neutralizing them individually with a laser—is ingenious and technically appealing.
However, a careful analysis from the perspectives of systems engineering, operational safety, industrial scalability, and regulatory acceptance reveals significant limitations that may compromise effectiveness, safety, and large-scale commercial adoption.
This white paper discusses these limitations and proposes an alternative architecture, based on hybrid analog–digital processing, infrared stereoscopic vision, hierarchical local and global AI, and multiple synchronized low-power lasers, capable of overcoming current technical bottlenecks, reducing systemic risks, and paving the way for medical, agricultural, and industrial applications with high social and economic impact.
